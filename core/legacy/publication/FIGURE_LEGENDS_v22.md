# Main figure legends

**Fig. 1 | WitnessCell links measured information to complete predictions and partner increments.** **a,** Allowed single perturbations, controls, training combinations and fixed annotations support a complete response core and an explicit partner component. The core measures or estimates endpoint responses, composes them nonlinearly and refines endpoints using training combinations. Measurement-reference calibration is applied where supported by the experiment; it is not retrospectively assigned to the earlier benchmark implementations. The partner representation and adjustment are fitted from permitted training observations. **b,** The framework contains separately fitted implementations with distinct evidence scopes. The released core, matched RNA kernel, growth matrix, program extension, H1 calibration and H1 shared-feature extension are not one unchanged predictor. SF12 is the manuscript alias for the archived 12-coefficient shared-feature model. **c,** Complete-response error measures delivered predictive accuracy; endpoint-eliminated error measures performance outside the free endpoint-additive space defined by query identity. The projection is an evaluation operator, not a query-batch-dependent inference rule. **d,** Measurement references, repeated observations, combination coverage and frozen evaluations constrain different parts of the construction and its interpretation.

**Fig. 2 | Combination-trained endpoint correction improves complete responses without changing endpoint-eliminated prediction.** **a,** The endpoint-incidence kernel gives \(\delta_Q=\gamma K_{QT}(K_{TT}+\eta I)^{-1}R_T=X_Q\Theta_T\). Consequently, \(P_Q\delta_Q=0\) for every fitted coefficient and training budget. This restriction concerns the specified residual branch; the complete nonlinear core can produce nonzero contrasts. **b,** Error reductions of each released v14 prediction relative to its own jointly selected composition backbone, with errors pooled across three released splits per dataset. Complete-response reductions are 5.74%, 31.94%, 0.066% and 1.23% for Norman, Wessels, Schmidt and Replogle_exp6. Additional endpoint-projected gains are numerically zero. Combination observations therefore make the complete model better through reusable endpoint estimation. **c,** Projected prediction error divided by projected observed-response energy for all twelve splits. One denotes zero prediction in the contrast space. Final and paired-backbone ratios overlap, with a maximum difference of \(6.34\times10^{-8}\), within saved-array precision. The analysis contains 540 combination–seed records and 212 summed within-split projected dimensions. These dimensions count evaluable linear contrasts, not independent interactions or biological replications. The panels retain the published readout axes; non-target and standardized sensitivity analyses are in Extended Data Fig. 1. Independently selected saturation-only predictions are separate controls from each model's paired backbone.

**Fig. 3 | The original response core provides competitive prediction across released transcriptomic benchmarks.** **a,** Mean top-100 operation rank under the released aggregation; the first eight of sixteen entries are displayed, with the full table in Source Data. Lower ranks are better. WitnessCell has rank 3.803 and scouter 5.411. **b,** WitnessCell's metric-specific positions among the sixteen entries. The distributional metrics use the released mean-based adapter with training-control variation, rather than a learned conditional cell distribution. **c,d,** Non-target MSE (**c**) and WMSE (**d**) reductions of the frozen final predictor against shared-endpoint additivity or independently selected saturation in Norman and Wessels. Points are gains calculated from the predeclared geometric mean of three split-specific loss ratios. Bars are 95% pair-bootstrap intervals from 5,000 draws carrying every record of a canonical pair together across seeds. Norman comprises 208 query–split records from 118 pairs; Wessels comprises 181 records from 106 pairs. The two targeted-gene coordinates are removed and evaluation weights renormalized within the retained coordinates. Norman's MSE improvement over shared-endpoint additivity is accompanied by near-zero WMSE gain; both outcomes are retained. **e,** Scope of the historical benchmark and its combination-only weighted estimate. The population comprises 540 combination–seed records and 114 unseen singles. The 540-record final/additive WMSE ratio of 0.7513 has the original condition-cluster interval 0.7031–0.8019. This historical weighted result includes target coordinates and retains its original cell-half truth convention, whereas **c,d** use the separate full-cell non-target evaluation. Repeated records across seeds are not independent biological replicates.

**Fig. 4 | Explicit partner learning improves held-out RNA contrasts.** **a,** Matched comparison of 55 unique combinations, 4,995 non-target readouts, five outer folds, 11 fixed within-fold rectangle contrasts and 15 endpoint-free dimensions. All methods use the same allowed outer observations and evaluation queries; their inner selection procedures are specified in Methods. M0 combines measured single effects, saturation and endpoint correction. M1 retains this complete baseline and adds the partner kernel; R scales the M1 − M0 increment with training-derived readout weights. **b,** Reduction in mean rectangle prediction error relative to M0. GEARS and scouter bars summarize mean loss across three optimization seeds; open points and horizontal ranges show individual seed values and their minimum–maximum range. These ranges describe optimization variability conditional on the same biological task. **c,** Endpoint-projected error normalized by projected truth energy. The dashed line denotes zero-contrast prediction. R has a ratio of 0.865, compared with 1.019 for M0, 0.918 for M1, 0.976 for GEARS and 1.0004 for scouter. GEARS also predicts part of the evaluated contrast signal; R achieves the largest reduction under this protocol. **d,** Error reductions for all eleven rectangles, indexed by one-based outer fold and within-fold rectangle. GEARS and scouter use seed-mean error. Displayed values are rounded to integer percentages; signed values near zero are retained in the source table. R improves nine of eleven rectangles relative to M0 and all eleven relative to M1. **e,** M1 and R contrast gains within each outer fold; R is positive in all five folds, with the third fold close to zero. Rectangles, folds and seeds share biological observations, so their agreement describes consistency within this panel. Complete-response results and fold-specific neural error contributions are shown in Extended Data Fig. 2.

**Fig. 5 | Training-measurement control and combination observations shape the added predictive capability.** **a,** Held-out RNA rectangle-error reductions relative to M0 for unit-weight M1, a training-selected uniform correction and readout-weighted R: 9.44%, 9.70% and 15.96%. All use the same fitted M0 and partner increment; the construction weights differ. **b,** Exact equivalence between the original cross-half weighting rule and a function of half-mean and half-difference energies. \(Z_1,Z_2\) are projected training half-response matrices. The two formulas describe one shrinkage rule, so the algebraically equivalent amplitude/noise form is not treated as a separate source of information or an independent predictive control. **c,** K562 complete-response gains beyond the M0 refitted at each observation budget. Low and high training sets contain 421–422 and 2,317–2,318 pairs per outer fold, respectively, and predict the same 632 distinct evaluation pairs across three folds. Rank-one gains are 0.27% and 11.03%; spectral gains are −0.29% and 11.39%. **d,** The corresponding endpoint-projected gains. At high budget, the reductions are 11.68% for rank one and 10.67% for spectral shrinkage. Each point is a ratio formed after pooling the relevant squared errors; both repeat-specific outcomes are retained in Extended Data Fig. 4. **e,** Cumulative share of positive RNA readout contributions, ranking each model's readouts separately. The ten leading readouts supply 82.21% of M1's positive reductions and 92.13% of R's, before subtracting detrimental contributions. This is a descriptive concentration analysis on the full 4,995-readout evaluation, not a preselected program endpoint. **f,** K562 information conditions. All eighty endpoints are represented and the endpoint-incidence design has rank eighty at both budgets, whereas median training degree increases from approximately ten to 58–59. Additional observations reduce the baseline's own error by 10.12%; the gains in **c,d** are measured beyond that separately refitted baseline. Count, degree and partner layout vary together. Weight-reassignment, scaling, complete RNA examples and detailed growth results are retained in Extended Data Figs. 3, 4, 6 and 8.

**Fig. 6 | Frozen growth prediction and independent-source RNA evaluation.** **a,** Following K562 development, the learning rules were frozen before fitting and evaluating the Jurkat task. Jurkat uses 79 genes, 2,464 training pairs and 617 held-out pairs with two growth-screen repeats. Parameters are fitted anew from Jurkat training observations. The assessment therefore tests the frozen procedure in another cell environment within the same original study. **b,c,** Complete-response and endpoint-projected gains relative to the repeat-specific M0. Rank one is the prespecified primary method; spectral shrinkage is secondary. Open circles show each repeat and filled diamonds show pooled squared-error ratios. The primary complete-response gain is 6.43%, with 4.45% and 8.88% in the two repeats; its projected gain is 6.18%. **d,e,** Held-out growth responses predicted by the frozen primary rank-one workflow. **d,** TTI2 paired with PGK1 or OGFOD1, shown separately in both growth-screen repeats. The correction makes TTI2 + PGK1 more negative and TTI2 + OGFOD1 less negative, improving both predictions in both repeats. **e,** MED1 + RPL10, for which each repeat also improves. Crosses and short horizontal segments, observed; grey squares, M0; blue diamonds, M0 plus the rank-one partner term. Growth phenotype \(\gamma\) is control-centered log2 enrichment per population doubling; more negative values indicate stronger relative depletion. Symbols and short segments display individual repeat values. Cases were selected post hoc from the evaluated pool to illustrate the saved predictions. The two TTI2 pairs show response differences while retaining the PGK1 and OGFOD1 endpoint terms; projected gains in **c** separately assess prediction beyond those free endpoint terms. Extended Data Fig. 5 reports gene-deletion sensitivity and baseline-averaging controls; Extended Data Fig. 7 places the cases within the complete test pool and includes an adverse example. All repeat-specific values are in Supplementary Table S10.

**f,** Separate-source Dixit RNA: complete and endpoint-eliminated increments of the training-selected extension relative to its own M0; inset annotation reports the complete core relative to additive. The partner primary endpoint did not improve. **g,** Fixed G2M-program complete and projected error ratios to M0, including measured-endpoint additivity. The extension improved this secondary score over M0 while remaining worse than additivity on complete-program error. RNA points are pooled over 45 held-out pairs, ten endpoints and fifteen contrast dimensions; they do not inherit the Jurkat repeat structure.

**Fig. 7 | Measurement calibration improves complete-response prediction while additional partner gains remain task dependent in H1 development.** **a,** Mean squared errors of the native H1 M0 under four input preparations, evaluated against the same fixed seven-library composition in 21 eligible held-out combinations. Errors average the two technical-half references and then conditions over five outer folds and 37,292 non-target RNA coordinates. Labels show reductions relative to raw cell-weighted input. The seven-library calibration reduces error by a further 2.73% relative to direct seven-library standardization. The nine-library arm uses extra source-library information; its advantage over direct standardization does not isolate the offset estimator. Technical halves and overlapping folds are not independent biological replications. **b,** Complete-response and endpoint-eliminated gains of SF12 relative to its task-specific M0 in every metadata-qualified four-combination holdout task. All five complete-response gains are positive; three partner gains are positive. **c,** Identity contrasts for the five tasks. A plus or minus denotes a coefficient of +1/2 or −1/2; blank cells have zero coefficient. The tasks share eight distinct combination edges and the contrast matrix has rank three. Q4 is the previously examined positive development block. **d,** Pooled SF12 partner gains after removing each task, without refitting or reselection. The gain changes sign when Q5 is omitted; these points describe sensitivity, not confidence intervals. **e,** Aggregate full-response and partner error reductions for SF12 and the frozen case-conditioned shrinkage candidate (archived as `case_R`). Full-response losses are balanced across the eight unique query edges before forming ratios; partner losses pool squared four-corner errors across tasks. The case candidate's initial full-response advantage does not generalize across this task set and it is not promoted. Both H1 analyses use historically examined development data; neither constitutes new-source confirmation. Full protocols and numerical values are in Supplementary Notes 11–12 and Tables S18–S19.
