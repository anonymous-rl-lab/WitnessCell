# Main figure legends

## Figure 1 | WitnessCell learns and separately controls endpoint and partner corrections

**a,** Released transcriptomic core. Measured or inferred single responses enter nonlinear composition; permitted combination measurements supply endpoint residual transfer. The benchmark evaluates this core configuration. **b,** The four-pair contrast removes contributions depending only on an endpoint. The full query projector $P_Q=I-X_QX_Q^+$ removes the corresponding endpoint span. **c,** RNA and functional extensions learn additional partner responses through separately fitted pair-kernel and residual-matrix constructions. They retain their own baselines and parameter estimates. Shared SF12/V4 functional functions provide the portable alternative to gene-indexed matrices (Supplementary Note 22); these are separately fitted correction generators. **d,** Endpoint residual transfer can change complete response but obeys $P_Q\delta_Q=0$. Explicit partner learners can change the projected response, whose predictive gain requires a separate held-out test. The original twelve-graph numerical check remains in Supplementary Table S9. **e,** Component control retains $(I-P_Q)D$ and applies an internally fitted coefficient to $P_QD$. Query identities define the operation; query outcomes enter only evaluation. This is a fixed-panel transformation, and the scientific results are the separate evaluations shown in subsequent figures.

## Figure 2 | Joint interventions resolve target ambiguities left by single interventions

**a,** The specified causal system has a binary source X and six mediators. Upstream intervention semantics are known; response coefficients and the active downstream world are hidden from estimation. Every candidate contains affine mediator terms and a direct-source term, with at most one gate from the declared family. The four generating worlds are direct-only and gates A, B and C. **b,** The informative and comparison panels add four different joint interventions to the same control and seven singles. They have identical endpoint degrees and observation budgets: twelve conditions with 256 source-stratified observations each. The query pairs AC, AF, CE and EF are absent from both panels. **c,** Population AB and BC interaction contrasts, scaled by source probability $p=0.5$, and the unmeasured query contrast $\Delta$. In particular, gate B creates measured interaction signals while its requested target remains zero. **d,** Equal-world target MSE of candidate least squares on each panel and a strong contrast-GLS reference on the informative panel. Points are averages across 1,000 paired noise seeds; whiskers are 95% Student-$t$ Monte Carlo intervals with 999 degrees of freedom. The dashed line is the estimator-independent equal-world risk floor of 0.0625 for the comparison panel. The 92.84% reduction compares informative-panel and comparison-panel least squares. The GLS difference is unresolved. Identification and confidence statements are conditional on the declared mechanism family; the enlarged-family counterexample is given in Supplementary Note 2.

## Figure 3 | WitnessCell leads aggregate evaluation of unmeasured combinations

**a,** Mean operation ranks for all sixteen eligible entries under the original top-100, six-metric rule. Open points show all 654 operation–seed records; filled points show the 540 double-perturbation records. Rows follow the original full-track order. The subset analysis keeps every metric, eligible method and operation score unchanged. WitnessCell's double-only mean rank is 3.515; the other 114 records are unseen singles, on which WitnessCell ranks second (Supplementary Table S25). **b,** Sixteen-method positions within the double-only exposure subgroups, aligned to panel a. Columns show neither, one or both endpoints seen in the released split, with 43, 374 and 123 records. Numbers are positions, not mean ranks. Some pair identities occur in more than one subgroup across split seeds. **c,** WitnessCell-minus-reference paired differences in mean operation rank for doubles. Whiskers are central 95% intervals from 20,000 bootstrap draws over 287 dataset–pair clusters, carrying repeated seed records together. Lower values favor WitnessCell. All fifteen reference intervals are shown in Supplementary Fig. S2. These are pointwise conditional intervals, not simultaneous confidence statements. All four dataset decompositions and native-scale losses are retained in Supplementary Notes 13 and 14. **d,** Relative mean Top-100 MSE reduction against Scouter in each double-perturbation dataset, computed from native-scale operation scores. Positive values denote lower WitnessCell MSE; negative values denote higher MSE. Bars are point estimates, not uncertainty intervals. Native losses and six-metric operation ranks measure different aspects of prediction. The original support-budget analysis is retained in Supplementary Fig. S3; weaker Wasserstein and common-DEG ranks remain in Supplementary Table S6. No full cell-distribution claim is inferred from the mean-based adapter.

## Figure 4 | Functional partner learning and selective control of its correction

**a,** K562 complete and partner gains at the two observed training budgets. Low pools contain 421–422 pairs and are nested within high pools of 2,317–2,318 pairs; the same fold's queries are unchanged. Filled circles show complete error and open squares partner error. Color distinguishes primary rank-one and secondary spectral corrections. Losses pool three folds and two measured repeats against the corresponding same-budget M0. No intermediate threshold is estimated. **b,** Jurkat primary rank-one gains on 617 queries, following procedure freezing and refitting on 2,464 measured pairs among 79 endpoints. Large filled diamonds show pooled complete and partner gains; small open points show the two measured repeats. Points are not confidence intervals. The secondary model and original conditional gene-reweighting intervals remain in Supplementary Table S15. **c,d,** The same previously selected Jurkat illustration: TTI2/ACTR10 backgrounds crossed with PGK1/OGFOD1. Panel c shows repeat-mean observed, baseline and corrected responses; panel d shows the signed four-corner contrast, with large points for means and small points for individual repeats. ACTR10 is alphabetically first among four eligible backgrounds; all are retained in Table S35. Units are control-centred log2 enrichment normalized by population doublings. **e,f,** Shen complete-response and partner gains for the original correction, whole attenuation and component control. Panel e shows retention of complete-response benefit; the exact preservation property concerns the endpoint contribution. Each cell background uses the same M0, original D and training-derived coefficient across policies. Baseline gain is zero. Whole and component attenuation give identical partner predictions; only the latter retains the original endpoint correction. These bars are retrospective point estimates, not independent confirmation or uncertainty intervals. Shen slopes are per day, and no absolute errors are pooled with Horlbeck. Table S37 supplies all 55 eligible configurations and Supplementary Fig. S4 gives repeat and panel checks. The original Shen partner outcomes remain adverse; small adjusted pooled gains do not hold positively across both repeats. **g,h,** Target-side calibration of the frozen HeLa- and 293T-derived SF12 partner functions in the target Han K562 screen. HeLa and 293T label the source models, not additional Han cell backgrounds. Panel g reports partner-error reduction relative to using the same correction unchanged; panel h reports reduction relative to removing it. Teal diamonds show reconstructed outcomes and blue open circles published phenotypes. Values pool query squared errors over the five three-fold pair partitions before taking ratios; symbols are point estimates, not confidence intervals. Reconstructed and published panels contain 20,359 and 20,791 eligible pairs, respectively. Only the scalar strength is estimated from target calibration combinations; base weights remain fixed. The complete-response reductions over single-response addition for reconstructed outcomes are 0.187% and 0.465%, respectively. The additive endpoint contribution is unchanged by construction. These two positive configurations are shown here; all six portable configurations, both readouts and partition sensitivity appear in Supplementary Fig. S5 and Tables S44–S49. The analysis follows earlier Han access. The two reconstructed repeats share controls, and published responses derive from the same study.

## Figure 5 | Readout shrinkage reduces adverse RNA corrections

**a,b,** Complete non-target RNA MSE (a; logarithmic axis) and rectangle MSE (b) in the matched 55-pair, five-fold CaRPool panel. All methods use the same 4,995 readouts and eleven same-fit rectangles. The evaluation averages eleven potentially overlapping rectangles. The 212 contrast dimensions in the separate core analysis do not describe this panel's sample size. Filled neural points are means of three optimization-seed losses; open points are the three individual seed values, and connecting lines show their range, not a confidence interval. Fixed models have one realization. These values do not score an ensemble-mean prediction. R reduces rectangle error by 15.96% relative to M0. **c,** Per-rectangle errors of M0 and R. The diagonal denotes equal error; nine of eleven rectangles improve. Rectangles can share pairs or endpoints and are not independent biological replicates. **d,** Positive reduction, adverse increment and net reduction in squared error for M1, uniform shrinkage and R. Each readout contribution is first summed over the eleven rectangles; positive and negative contributions are then separated. Every component uses the same total M0 SSE denominator. Adverse increments are drawn below zero and diamonds show net gains. R's total positive error reduction is 84.71% of M1's, and its total adverse increment is 85.25% lower. The positive and adverse sets are determined separately for each model. This is a descriptive decomposition of the same development panel. Permutation and random-increment controls remain in Supplementary Note 6. **e,** Cumulative positive error reduction, ranking readouts separately for each method. The first ten readouts account for 92.13% of R's positive contributions. Negative contributions are retained in all aggregate losses; this curve does not plot a share of net gain. **f,** Rectangle gains relative to M0 under the primary raw scale, outer-training across-condition variance standardization and deletion of the previously fixed ten-readout set. The latter analyses delimit the breadth of the raw-scale benefit. All model selections, adverse results and independent-source checks are retained in Supplementary Notes 5, 6 and 10.
