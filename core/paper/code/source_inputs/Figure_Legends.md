# Main figure legends

## Figure 1 | A predictor with separately testable endpoint and partner capabilities

**a,** Released transcriptomic core. Measured or inferred single effects form a nonlinear response baseline; combinations supply regularized endpoint residual transfer. Hyperparameters use permitted training/validation observations. **b,** Unsigned endpoint incidence and a four-pair contrast. The projector removes arbitrary additive endpoint contributions; the released endpoint residual obeys $P_Q\delta_Q=0$. This identity applies to the residual branch, while the nonlinear baseline can already contain partner-sensitive variation. Endpoint refers to intervention identity, not only the targeted RNA coordinate. **c,** Two separately fitted extensions add partner capacity: the RNA predictor uses a projected response-similarity kernel and training-half readout weights, whereas the functional predictor uses a symmetric residual matrix with training-only model selection. Each extension has its own baseline and parameters. The shared diagram describes computational roles, not one parameter set or an experimentally demonstrated causal chain between all results. See Supplementary Tables S1, S29 and S30.

## Figure 2 | Joint interventions resolve target ambiguities left by single interventions

**a,** The specified causal system has a binary source X and six mediators. Upstream intervention semantics are known; response coefficients and the active downstream world are hidden from estimation. Every candidate contains affine mediator terms and a direct-source term, with at most one gate from the declared family. The four generating worlds are direct-only and gates A, B and C. **b,** The informative and comparison panels add four different joint interventions to the same control and seven singles. They have identical endpoint degrees and observation budgets: twelve conditions with 256 source-stratified observations each. The query pairs AC, AF, CE and EF are absent from both panels. **c,** Population AB and BC interaction contrasts, scaled by source probability $p=0.5$, and the unmeasured query contrast $\Delta$. In particular, gate B creates measured interaction signals while its requested target remains zero. **d,** Equal-world target MSE of candidate least squares on each panel and a strong contrast-GLS reference on the informative panel. Points are averages across 1,000 paired noise seeds; whiskers are 95% Student-$t$ Monte Carlo intervals with 999 degrees of freedom. The dashed line is the estimator-independent equal-world risk floor of 0.0625 for the comparison panel. The 92.84% reduction compares informative-panel and comparison-panel least squares. The GLS difference is unresolved. Identification and confidence statements are conditional on the declared mechanism family; the enlarged-family counterexample is given in Supplementary Note 2.

## Figure 3 | The released predictor leads the full track and double-perturbation subset

**a,** Mean operation ranks for all sixteen eligible entries under the original top-100, six-metric rule. Open points show all 654 operation–seed records; filled points show the 540 double-perturbation records. Rows follow the original full-track order. The subset analysis keeps every metric, eligible method and operation score unchanged. WitnessCell's double-only mean rank is 3.515; the other 114 records are unseen singles, on which WitnessCell ranks second (Supplementary Table S25). **b,** Sixteen-method positions within the double-only exposure subgroups, aligned to panel a. Columns show neither, one or both endpoints seen in the released split, with 43, 374 and 123 records. Numbers are positions, not mean ranks. Some pair identities occur in more than one subgroup across split seeds. **c,** WitnessCell-minus-reference paired differences in mean operation rank for doubles. Whiskers are central 95% intervals from 20,000 bootstrap draws over 287 dataset–pair clusters, carrying repeated seed records together. Lower values favor WitnessCell. All fifteen reference intervals are shown in Supplementary Fig. S2. These are pointwise conditional intervals, not simultaneous confidence statements. All four dataset decompositions and native-scale losses are retained in Supplementary Notes 13 and 14. **d,** Relative mean Top-100 MSE reduction against Scouter in each double-perturbation dataset, computed from native-scale operation scores. Positive values denote lower WitnessCell MSE; negative values denote higher MSE. Bars are point estimates, not uncertainty intervals. Native losses and six-metric operation ranks measure different aspects of prediction. The original support-budget analysis is retained in Supplementary Fig. S3; weaker Wasserstein and common-DEG ranks remain in Supplementary Table S6. No full cell-distribution claim is inferred from the mean-based adapter.

## Figure 4 | Functional-screen completion and frozen-procedure partner prediction

**a,** Functional-screen workflow: two-repeat single and measured pair phenotypes supply separately fitted baselines and a shared residual correction; training-only selection yields predictions for unmeasured pairs. Revised K562 development uses eighty genes and 632 held-out pairs, excluding the original registered test pool. The procedure is frozen before Jurkat fitting and scoring; coefficients are refitted on its 2,464 training pairs. Jurkat has 79 genes and 617 held-out pairs with two measured repeats, within the same source study. **b,** K562 gains of the rank-one and spectral corrections relative to their own same-budget M0. Low and high training budgets contain 421–422 and 2,317–2,318 pairs per fold. Complete and endpoint-eliminated losses pool squared errors across three folds and both repeats before forming ratios. Lines connect the two measured budgets; they do not estimate an interpolated learning curve. **c,** Jurkat pooled complete-response gains. Rank one is primary and spectral correction secondary. Whiskers are central 95% conditional gene-reweighting intervals from 10,000 draws with predictions fixed; they are sensitivity summaries rather than population confidence guarantees. **d,** Complete-response gain in each measured repeat and pooled endpoint-eliminated gain on the same 617 pairs. **e,** Two descriptively selected pairs already discussed in the main text. Points show mean observed response, M0 and rank-one predictions across the two source repeats on a common original-unit axis. Arrows connect M0 to its corrected prediction. The shared TTI2 endpoint receives oppositely directed corrections with different partners. Growth units are control-centred log2 enrichment normalized by population doublings. All-pair results, numerical optimization checks and conditional sensitivities are in Supplementary Note 7.

## Figure 5 | An explicit RNA partner increment improves held-out contrasts with concentrated readout benefits

**a,b,** Complete non-target RNA MSE (a; logarithmic axis) and rectangle MSE (b) in the matched 55-pair, five-fold CaRPool panel. All methods use the same 4,995 readouts and eleven same-fit rectangles. Filled neural points are means of three optimization-seed losses; open points are the three individual seed values, and connecting lines show their range, not a confidence interval. Fixed models have one realization. These values do not score an ensemble-mean prediction. R reduces rectangle error by 15.96% relative to M0. **c,** Per-rectangle errors of M0 and R. The diagonal denotes equal error; nine of eleven rectangles improve. Rectangles can share pairs or endpoints and are not independent biological replicates. **d,** Positive reduction, adverse increment and net reduction in squared error for M1, uniform shrinkage and R. Each readout contribution is first summed over the eleven rectangles; positive and negative contributions are then separated. Every component uses the same total M0 SSE denominator. Adverse increments are drawn below zero and diamonds show net gains. R retains 84.71% of M1’s positive reduction while reducing adverse increments by 85.25%. This is a descriptive decomposition of the same development panel. Permutation and random-increment controls remain in Supplementary Note 6. **e,** Cumulative positive error reduction, ranking readouts separately for each method. The first ten readouts account for 92.13% of R's positive contributions. Negative contributions are retained in all aggregate losses; this curve does not plot a share of net gain. **f,** Rectangle gains relative to M0 under the primary raw scale, outer-training across-condition variance standardization and deletion of the previously fixed ten-readout set. The latter analyses delimit the breadth of the raw-scale benefit. All model selections, adverse results and independent-source checks are retained in Supplementary Notes 5, 6 and 10.
