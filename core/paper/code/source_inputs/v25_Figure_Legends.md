# Main figure legends

## Figure 1 | WitnessCell constructs response predictions from measured experimental support

**a,** Inputs and the released transcriptomic predictor. Controls define the response reference; permitted singles provide measured effects or training-derived estimates for missing endpoints. Nonlinear composition produces the baseline, and measured combinations supply residuals for regularized endpoint transfer. In this schematic, $\delta$ denotes the unscaled transferred correction and $\gamma$ its fitted amplitude. The released configuration supplies the formal benchmark in Fig. 3. **b,** An illustrative four-pair incidence matrix shows how query identities define the projector $P_Q=I-X_QX_Q^+$. The endpoint-transfer branch lies in the column space of $X_Q$ and cancels under $P_Q$, while nonlinear composition can already contain partner-sensitive variation. Matrix entries are exact identity indicators, not simulated expression values. **c,** The separately fitted RNA extension constructs a symmetric kernel from single-response similarities, removes training endpoint directions, fits a ridge correction and applies readout-specific shrinkage from complementary training cell halves. M0 includes the relevant baseline and endpoint refinement; R adds the weighted partner correction. The figure describes these computational constructions, rather than an experimentally validated causal chain between their outcomes. Growth uses a separate residual-matrix implementation. Details and proofs are in Supplementary Notes 3–6.

## Figure 2 | Joint interventions resolve target ambiguities left by single interventions

**a,** The specified causal system has a binary source X and six mediators. Upstream intervention semantics are known; response coefficients and the active downstream world are hidden from estimation. Every candidate contains affine mediator terms and a direct-source term, with at most one gate from the declared family. The four generating worlds are direct-only and gates A, B and C. **b,** The informative and comparison panels add four different joint interventions to the same control and seven singles. They have identical endpoint degrees and observation budgets: twelve conditions with 256 source-stratified observations each. The query pairs AC, AF, CE and EF are absent from both panels. **c,** Population AB and BC interaction contrasts, scaled by source probability $p=0.5$, and the unmeasured query contrast $\Delta$. In particular, gate B creates measured interaction signals while its requested target remains zero. **d,** Equal-world target MSE of candidate least squares on each panel and a strong contrast-GLS reference on the informative panel. Points are averages across 1,000 paired noise seeds; whiskers are 95% Student-$t$ Monte Carlo intervals with 999 degrees of freedom. The dashed line is the estimator-independent equal-world risk floor of 0.0625 for the comparison panel. The 92.84% reduction compares informative-panel and comparison-panel least squares. The GLS difference is unresolved. Identification and confidence statements are conditional on the declared mechanism family; the enlarged-family counterexample is given in Supplementary Note 2.

## Figure 3 | The released predictor leads the full track and double-perturbation subset

**a,** Mean operation ranks for all sixteen eligible entries under the original top-100, six-metric rule. Open points show all 654 operation–seed records; filled points show the 540 double-perturbation records. Rows follow the original full-track order. The subset analysis keeps every metric, eligible method and operation score unchanged. WitnessCell's double-only mean rank is 3.515; the other 114 records are unseen singles, on which WitnessCell ranks second (Supplementary Table S25). **b,** Sixteen-method positions within the double-only exposure subgroups, aligned to panel a. Columns show neither, one or both endpoints seen in the released split, with 43, 374 and 123 records. Numbers are positions, not mean ranks. Some pair identities occur in more than one subgroup across split seeds. **c,** WitnessCell-minus-reference paired differences in mean operation rank for doubles. Whiskers are central 95% intervals from 20,000 bootstrap draws over 287 dataset–pair clusters, carrying repeated seed records together. Lower values favor WitnessCell. All fifteen reference comparisons, all four dataset decompositions and native-scale losses are retained in Supplementary Note 13 and its source tables. **d,** Wessels gains over the fixed nonlinear backbone as the training-combination residual library increases from one quarter to one half and full support. Shaded regions are the archived 95% canonical-pair bootstrap intervals from 5,000 draws. This tests endpoint refinement of complete responses. The unchanged metric-specific profile, including weaker Wasserstein and common-DEG positions, remains in Supplementary Table S6; distributional metrics use the benchmark's mean-based adapter.

## Figure 4 | An explicit RNA partner increment improves held-out contrasts with concentrated readout benefits

**a,b,** Complete non-target RNA MSE (a; logarithmic axis) and rectangle MSE (b) in the matched 55-pair, five-fold CaRPool panel. All methods use the same 4,995 readouts and eleven same-fit rectangles. Filled neural points are means of three optimization-seed losses; open points are the three individual seed values, and connecting lines show their range, not a confidence interval. Fixed models have one realization. These values do not score an ensemble-mean prediction. R reduces rectangle error by 15.96% relative to M0. **c,** Per-rectangle errors of M0 and R. The diagonal denotes equal error; nine of eleven rectangles improve. Rectangles can share pairs or endpoints and are not independent biological replicates. **d,** Positive reduction, adverse increment and net reduction in squared error for M1, uniform shrinkage and R. Each readout contribution is first summed over the eleven rectangles; positive and negative contributions are then separated. Every component uses the same total M0 SSE denominator. Adverse increments are drawn below zero and diamonds show net gains. R retains 84.71% of M1’s positive reduction while reducing adverse increments by 85.25%. This is a descriptive decomposition of the same development panel. Permutation and random-increment controls remain in Supplementary Note 6. **e,** Cumulative positive error reduction, ranking readouts separately for each method. The first ten readouts account for 92.13% of R's positive contributions. Negative contributions are retained in all aggregate losses; this curve does not plot a share of net gain. **f,** Rectangle gains relative to M0 under the primary raw scale, outer-training across-condition variance standardization and deletion of the previously fixed ten-readout set. The latter analyses delimit the breadth of the raw-scale benefit. All model selections, adverse results and independent-source checks are retained in Supplementary Notes 5, 6 and 10.

## Figure 5 | Combination support enables functional growth prediction

**a,** Evaluation design. Revised K562 development uses eighty genes and 632 held-out pairs, separate from the original registered test pool. The selected procedure is frozen before Jurkat fitting and evaluation; coefficients are estimated from Jurkat training data. Jurkat includes 79 genes, 2,464 training pairs and 617 held-out pairs, each with two source repeats. **b,** K562 gains of the rank-one and spectral corrections relative to their own same-budget M0. Low and high training budgets contain 421–422 and 2,317–2,318 pairs per fold. Complete and endpoint-eliminated losses pool squared errors across three folds and both repeats before forming ratios. Lines connect the two measured budgets; they do not estimate an interpolated learning curve. **c,** Jurkat pooled complete-response gains. Rank one is primary and spectral correction secondary. Whiskers are central 95% conditional gene-reweighting intervals from 10,000 draws with predictions fixed; they are sensitivity summaries rather than population confidence guarantees. **d,** Complete-response gain in each measured repeat and pooled endpoint-eliminated gain on the same 617 pairs. **e,** Two descriptively selected pairs already discussed in the main text. Points show mean observed response, M0 and rank-one predictions across the two source repeats on a common original-unit axis. Arrows connect M0 to its corrected prediction. The shared TTI2 endpoint receives oppositely directed corrections with different partners. Growth units are control-centred log2 enrichment normalized by population doublings. All-pair results, numerical optimization checks and conditional sensitivities are in Supplementary Note 7.

## Figure 6 | Risk selection and complete retrospective partner-selection outcomes

**a,** PairPick use case. An anchor's single response defines a fixed transcriptional objective; candidate response predictions yield projected utilities and a partner recommendation. Held-out measurements assess achieved utility in the prescribed pool. **b,** A separate analytic-risk evaluation, not a filtering stage applied to PairPick. The fixed calibration threshold gives accepted/all-query MSE ratio 0.5343 at 49.48% pair-balanced coverage, using 213 records from 33 calibration-disjoint pair identities. The weighted complete-case follow-up gives 0.4775 at 48.95% coverage, using 207 records from 32 pairs. Whiskers are the recorded 95% pair-cluster bootstrap intervals. **c,** Regret for all 28 GSE220974 tasks and all five primary methods, with identical candidate pools and physical-pair holdouts. Rows are ordered by physical anchor, mode-specific anchor and fold, independently of outcome; white separators mark physical-anchor groups. Suffixes a/i denote activation/repression, and the number after the slash is the fold. Dots denote zero regret; nonzero values and the color scale use regret multiplied by 1,000. An inverse-hyperbolic-sine color normalization with linear width 5 makes smaller losses visible while retaining the full range. Neural entries score the mean of three seed predictions. The original design-table audit directly matches 15 of the 49 detected gene-mode labels, identifies one enhancer-only correspondence and leaves 33 unlisted as designed pairs. All tasks are retained as a detected-label demonstration; label matching does not verify cell-level constructs. **d,** R-G-minus-reference achieved-utility differences with 95% bootstrap intervals clustering the eight physical anchors. R-G and the archived PRESAGE adapter choose identically in all 28 tasks. Complete regret, hit, win/tie/loss and prediction-error tables are in Supplementary Tables S17–S19 and S28. These descriptive outcomes do not establish superiority across all methods or independent biological confirmation of intended constructs.
