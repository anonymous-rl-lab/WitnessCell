# Main figure legends

## Figure 1 | WitnessCell learns and separately controls endpoint and partner corrections

**a,** Released transcriptomic core. Measured or inferred single responses enter nonlinear composition; permitted combination measurements supply endpoint residual transfer. The benchmark evaluates this core configuration. **b,** The four-pair contrast removes contributions depending only on an endpoint. The full query projector $P_Q=I-X_QX_Q^+$ removes the corresponding endpoint span. **c,** RNA and functional extensions learn additional partner responses through separately fitted pair-kernel and residual-matrix constructions. They retain their own baselines and parameter estimates. **d,** Endpoint residual transfer can change complete response but obeys $P_Q\delta_Q=0$. Explicit partner learners can change the projected response, whose predictive gain requires a separate held-out test. The original twelve-graph numerical check remains in Supplementary Table S9. **e,** Component control retains $(I-P_Q)D$ and applies an internally fitted coefficient to $P_QD$. Query identities define the operation; query outcomes enter only evaluation. The transformation is defined on a fixed query panel; subsequent figures evaluate its predictive effect.

## Figure 2 | Joint interventions resolve target ambiguities left by single interventions

**a,** The specified causal system has a binary source X and six mediators. Upstream intervention semantics are known; response coefficients and the active downstream world are hidden from estimation. Every candidate contains affine mediator terms and a direct-source term, with at most one gate from the declared family. The four generating worlds are direct-only and gates A, B and C. **b,** The informative and comparison panels add four different joint interventions to the same control and seven singles. They have identical endpoint degrees and observation budgets: twelve conditions with 256 source-stratified observations each. The query pairs AC, AF, CE and EF are absent from both panels. **c,** Population AB and BC interaction contrasts, scaled by source probability $p=0.5$, and the unmeasured query contrast $\Delta$. In particular, gate B creates measured interaction signals while its requested target remains zero. **d,** Equal-world target MSE of candidate least squares on each panel and a strong contrast-GLS reference on the informative panel. Points are averages across 1,000 paired noise seeds; whiskers are 95% Student-$t$ Monte Carlo intervals with 999 degrees of freedom. The dashed line is the estimator-independent equal-world risk floor of 0.0625 for the comparison panel. The 92.84% reduction compares informative-panel and comparison-panel least squares. The GLS difference is unresolved. Identification and confidence statements are conditional on the declared mechanism family; the enlarged-family counterexample is given in Supplementary Note 2.

## Figure 3 | WitnessCell leads aggregate evaluation of unmeasured combinations

**a,** Mean operation ranks for all sixteen eligible entries under the original top-100, six-metric rule. Open points show all 654 operation–seed records; filled points show the 540 double-perturbation records. Rows follow the original full-track order. The subset analysis keeps every metric, eligible method and operation score unchanged. WitnessCell's double-only mean rank is 3.515; the other 114 records are unseen singles, on which WitnessCell ranks second (Supplementary Table S25). **b,** Sixteen-method positions within the double-only exposure subgroups, aligned to panel a. Columns show neither, one or both endpoints seen in the released split, with 43, 374 and 123 records. Numbers are positions, not mean ranks. Some pair identities occur in more than one subgroup across split seeds. **c,** WitnessCell-minus-reference paired differences in mean operation rank for doubles. Whiskers are central 95% intervals from 20,000 bootstrap draws over 287 dataset–pair clusters, carrying repeated seed records together. Lower values favor WitnessCell. All fifteen reference intervals are shown in Supplementary Fig. S2. These are pointwise conditional intervals, not simultaneous confidence statements. All four dataset decompositions and native-scale losses are reported in Supplementary Notes 13 and 14. **d,** Relative mean Top-100 MSE reduction against Scouter in each double-perturbation dataset, computed from native-scale operation scores. Positive values denote lower WitnessCell MSE; negative values denote higher MSE. Bars are point estimates, not uncertainty intervals. Native losses and six-metric operation ranks measure different aspects of prediction. The support-budget analysis is shown in Supplementary Fig. S3; Wasserstein and common-DEG ranks are reported in Supplementary Table S6. No full cell-distribution claim is inferred from the mean-based adapter.

## Figure 4 | Functional response gains, global references and selective control

Panels a–b show functional learning, c–d examine the source of its increment, e–f evaluate another study, and g–h examine prediction control. **a,** Original K562 complete and partner gains under nested low/high training support. Circles denote complete loss and open squares partner loss; teal and blue distinguish rank-one and secondary spectral correction. Gains pool all three folds and both measured repeats relative to the same-budget original baseline. **b,** Original Jurkat rank-one gains across 617 queries after procedure freezing and local parameter fitting; filled diamonds pool repeats and open circles show each repeat. Panels a and b use the original functional baseline. **c,** Rank-one partner gains relative to the original baseline (open circles) and the jointly refitted baseline with one unpenalized single-product coefficient per repeat (filled diamonds). K562 high-support gain changes from 11.68% to 11.51%; Jurkat changes from 6.18% to 0.41%. The matrix learner is refitted under the same inner grid and zero option. Each point uses its own baseline denominator; the connecting line is not an uncertainty interval. Table S54 additionally compares Jurkat final predictions on the original-baseline denominator and reports four fits using the same saturation-reference family as Han; K562 rank-one partner gain remains 11.32% under that reference. The table includes all configurations and absolute losses. **d,** K562 fold-0 fitted rank-one loadings versus mean single effects; teal marks the sixteen-gene descriptive mitochondrial set. The correlation is centered Pearson correlation of the actual fitted loadings, not of the training-residual matrix mode. Gene labels are illustrative; the source table includes all loadings. The sign of the rank-one vector is oriented toward the designated set and has no absolute interaction-sign interpretation. **e,f,** Native Han partner gains over the native functional baseline (e) and the internally selected nonlinear saturation reference (f). Filled teal diamonds denote reconstructed responses and open blue circles published phenotypes. Points pool SSE; segments span five partition estimates, not confidence intervals. Each partition has three whole-pair folds. The reconstructed panel contains 20,359 pairs and two end repeats; the published panel has 20,791 pairs and one aggregate response. The published saturation/rank-one configuration selects zero in every fold. Table S57 decomposes the complete saved correction into the single-product direction and its orthogonal remainder. **g,** Shen complete-response gains under original correction, whole attenuation and component control using the same internal partner coefficient. Component control retains endpoint refinement; these are complete, not partner, gains. **h,** Reconstructed Han native rank-one predictions: original correction, whole attenuation with an independently complete-loss-optimized internal coefficient $\gamma$, partner removal and component control. Filled circles show complete gains and open squares partner gains, relative to the same native baseline. Control changes neither the endpoint contribution nor the learned correction direction. Tables S45–S50 and Supplementary Fig. S6 report all readouts, baselines, learners and control outcomes. Native Han fitting and the matched global-reference sensitivity use previously accessed data; overlapping partitions and related readouts remain within their original studies.

## Figure 5 | Readout shrinkage reduces adverse RNA corrections

**a,b,** Complete non-target RNA MSE (a; logarithmic axis) and rectangle MSE (b) in the matched 55-pair, five-fold CaRPool panel. All methods use the same 4,995 readouts and eleven same-fit rectangles. The evaluation unit is one of eleven potentially overlapping rectangles; the separate core analysis contains 212 algebraic contrast dimensions. Filled neural points are means of three optimization-seed losses; open points are the three individual seed values, and connecting lines show their range, not a confidence interval. Fixed models have one realization. These values do not score an ensemble-mean prediction. R reduces rectangle error by 15.96% relative to M0. **c,** Per-rectangle errors of M0 and R. The diagonal denotes equal error; nine of eleven rectangles improve. Rectangles can share pairs or endpoints and are not independent biological replicates. **d,** Positive reduction, adverse increment and net reduction in squared error for M1, uniform shrinkage and R. Each readout contribution is first summed over the eleven rectangles; positive and negative contributions are then separated. Every component uses the same total M0 SSE denominator. Adverse increments are drawn below zero and diamonds show net gains. R's total positive error reduction is 84.71% of M1's, and its total adverse increment is 85.25% lower. The positive and adverse sets are determined separately for each model. This is a descriptive decomposition of the same development panel. Permutation and random-increment controls are reported in Supplementary Note 6. **e,** Cumulative positive error reduction, ranking readouts separately for each method. The first ten readouts account for 92.13% of R's positive contributions. Negative contributions are retained in all aggregate losses; this curve does not plot a share of net gain. **f,** Rectangle gains relative to M0 under the primary raw scale, outer-training across-condition variance standardization and deletion of the previously fixed ten-readout set. The latter analyses delimit the breadth of the raw-scale benefit. All model selections, adverse results and independent-source checks are reported in Supplementary Notes 5, 6 and 10.
